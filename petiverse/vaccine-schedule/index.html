Dog and cat vaccination schedule

Which vaccines puppies, kittens and adult pets need, and when. A plain summary of the WSAVA 2024 vaccination guidelines.

Your vet sets your pet's actual schedule. Local law, especially for rabies, may differ from this guide.

Core and non-core vaccines

Core vaccines protect against serious, widespread diseases, and every dog and cat should get them. Non-core vaccines depend on where and how your pet lives: going outdoors, boarding, other pets, travel.

  • Dogs. Combo vaccine (DHPPi): distemper, adenovirus (hepatitis) and parvovirus, core everywhere. Rabies: core where the disease is present or the law requires it. Leptospirosis: core in regions where it is common.
  • Cats. Combo vaccine (FVRCP): panleukopenia, herpesvirus and calicivirus (cat flu), core everywhere. Rabies: core where the disease is present or the law requires it. Feline leukemia (FeLV): core for cats under 1 year and for cats that go outdoors, in regions where the virus is common.
  • Non-core, by lifestyle. Kennel cough: for dogs that board, go to day care or meet many dogs. Your vet may suggest others for your region.

🐶 Puppy and dog vaccination schedule

6–8 weeksCombo vaccine (DHPPi): first shot.

Adult dogCombo vaccine (DHPPi): no more often than every 3 years. Leptospirosis and Kennel cough: usually yearly. Rabies: as local law and the vaccine require.

Adult with an unknown vaccine history

An adopted adult with no records does not start over like a puppy or kitten. Dogs usually need one core vaccine shot, cats two shots 2–4 weeks apart, plus rabies where required. After that, the adult schedule applies.

Deworming, flea and tick

These are not vaccines, but they belong on the same card. Deworming and Flea & tick: puppies and kittens need them often in their first months, and adults regularly for life. Your vet sets the plan for your pet's age, lifestyle and region.

Good to know

  • Puppy socialisation can start before the vaccine series ends, in clean places with healthy, vaccinated dogs.
  • Tiredness or a sore spot for a day after a vaccine is common. Swelling of the face, repeated vomiting or trouble breathing: call a vet right away.
  • Bring the vaccine card to every visit. Boarding, travel and grooming often ask for it.
  • Vaccines should not be given when they are not needed. Your vet weighs the risks and benefits for your pet.

Common questions

When should a puppy get its first vaccine?

At 6–8 weeks of age. The core vaccine is repeated every 2–4 weeks until 16 weeks or older, then once more at about 6 months.

When should a kitten get its first vaccine?

At 6–8 weeks of age. The core vaccine is repeated every 2–4 weeks until 16 weeks or older, then once more at about 6 months.

How often do adult dogs and cats need vaccines?

Core vaccines usually no more often than every 3 years. Some are yearly: leptospirosis and kennel cough in dogs, cat flu protection for cats at higher risk. Rabies follows local law.

Is the rabies vaccine required?

In many countries it is required by law, and it is core wherever rabies is present. The law sets the age and how often. Ask your vet.
