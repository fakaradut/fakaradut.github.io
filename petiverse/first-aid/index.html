First aid for cats and dogs

The first steps on the way to the vet. These cards help you act calmly; they never replace a vet.

📞 Call a vet or the nearest 24/7 clinic first and tell them you are on your way.

Suspected poisoning

Bring what they ate / the packaging. Do NOT induce vomiting. Wipe any residue from the mouth with a cloth. Call the vet and get going immediately.

Heatstroke

Move to shade/cool. Wet paws, belly and armpits with COOL water (never ice); use a fan if possible. Small sips of water are fine. Cool down on the way to the vet.

Choking

A panicking animal may bite — careful. Remove a visible object with tweezers/fingers only if SAFE; don't force it if you can't see it. If they can't breathe, rush to the nearest vet and call ahead.

Bleeding

Press a clean cloth/gauze firmly for 3–5 min without lifting to peek; add layers on top. No tourniquets. Keep pressing on the way to the vet.

Trouble breathing

Open the mouth; remove a visible object only if you can do it SAFELY — never blind-finger. Remove anything tight around the neck, keep them cool and calm, leave now.

Seizure

Don't touch or restrain; move away objects they could hit. Dim lights, time it. Keep them warm afterwards and go to the vet. Longer than 3 minutes: go immediately.

Hit by car / fall

Approach calmly; an animal in pain may bite. Move on a firm board/blanket in case of spinal injury; don't rock them in your arms. Press a clean cloth on any bleeding. Straight to the vet.

Burn

Cool the area with cool (not icy) water for 10 min. No creams, toothpaste or yogurt. Cover with a clean damp cloth and see the vet.

These cards are not veterinary advice; they cover first response on the way. The goal is always to reach a vet as fast as possible. Never give human medication to an animal.

In Petiverse these cards work offline, and Emergency mode shows the nearest vets with 24/7 clinics first.
