Is xylitol toxic to dogs and cats?

By Moon Arc Works · Updated November 23, 2026

🐱 Cats

Risky

Risky. Small amounts may cause problems; avoid it and ask your vet if eaten.

🐶 Dogs

Toxic

Toxic. If eaten, call a vet now, even if your pet seems fine.

Yes, for dogs xylitol is one of the most dangerous household poisons. This sweetener, also listed as birch sugar or E967, is in sugar-free gum, mints, sweets, baked goods, some peanut butters, toothpaste and chewable vitamins. Even small amounts, such as a few pieces of gum, can be serious for a dog.

Why it is harmful

In dogs, xylitol causes a large release of insulin, and blood sugar can fall dangerously within 10 to 60 minutes. Larger amounts can also damage the liver within a day or two. Read labels: a product may say sugar-free on the front and list xylitol only in the ingredients.

Signs to watch for

  • Vomiting
  • Weakness, wobbly walking, tiredness
  • Trembling or seizures
  • Collapse
  • Later: yellow gums or eyes, bruising or bleeding (liver damage)

Cats and dogs

The effect is well documented in dogs. In cats it is less clear, and cats rarely eat sweet things; any xylitol eaten by a cat is still a reason to call the vet.

What to do

🔴 Vet, right now

Your dog ate anything containing xylitol, or something sugar-free whose label you can't check. Go now, even if your dog seems normal, and bring the packaging.

🟠 Vet today

Your cat ate a small amount of a xylitol product and seems normal. Call your vet today for advice.

🟢 Watch at home

Not for dogs. For a cat, only if your vet has said watching at home is fine.

My pet ate it. What now?

  1. Call your vet or the nearest 24/7 clinic. Say what was eaten, how much and when.
  2. Do not try to make your pet vomit unless a vet tells you to.
  3. Keep the packaging or a photo of the plant to show the vet.
  4. Watch for vomiting, drooling, weakness, shaking or trouble breathing.

This page is general information, not a substitute for a vet. Only a vet who knows your pet can judge the risk.
