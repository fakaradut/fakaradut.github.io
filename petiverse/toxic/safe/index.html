Safe foods and plants for dogs and cats

By Moon Arc Works · Updated November 23, 2026

These foods are generally safe for both dogs and cats in small, plain portions, and these plants are considered non-toxic. Safe does not mean unlimited: treats should stay a small part of the daily diet.

Before you share

  • Plain only: no salt, spices, butter, sauces, onion or garlic.
  • Small pieces, a little at a time; try a new food once and watch for an upset stomach.
  • Treats should make up only a small part of what your pet eats in a day.
  • If your pet has a health condition such as kidney disease, diabetes or pancreatitis, ask your vet first.
  • A non-toxic plant can still cause a mild upset stomach, and garden plants may carry pesticides or fertilizer.

Safe foods

Apple (no seeds)

Apple slices are a crunchy, low-fat treat. Remove the core and seeds: the seeds contain small amounts of a cyanide compound and the core can cause choking.

Banana (small)

A few slices without the peel are fine. Bananas are high in sugar, so keep portions small.

Boiled chicken (plain)

Plain, boneless, skinless boiled chicken is gentle on the stomach. No salt, spices, onion or garlic, and never cooked bones.

Boiled egg

Fully cooked plain egg is a good source of protein. Avoid raw egg and don't add salt or butter.

Bread (small, plain)

A small piece of plain baked bread is fine. Avoid raisin, garlic, onion and nut breads, and never give raw dough.

Carrot

Raw or cooked carrot is low in calories. Cut it into small pieces or thin sticks so it can't be swallowed whole.

Cheese (small, low-salt)

Small cubes of plain, low-salt hard cheese make a good occasional treat. Avoid blue cheese, spicy cheese and cheese with garlic or onion; some adult pets get diarrhea from dairy.

Fish (cooked, boneless)

Cooked, boneless fish such as salmon or white fish is a good source of protein. Avoid raw fish as a habit, small bones, and fish cooked with garlic, onion or a lot of salt.

Peanut butter (xylitol-free)

Plain peanut butter is a favorite treat for dogs. Check the label: it must not contain xylitol (birch sugar). Choose one without added salt and give a little, as it is very fatty.

Rice (cooked)

Plain cooked rice is easy to digest. Serve it without salt, butter or sauces.

Watermelon (seedless)

Seedless flesh without the rind is a refreshing treat. The rind and a lot of seeds can upset the stomach or block the gut.

Pet-safe plants

African violet

Non-toxic to dogs and cats. A nibble may at most cause a mild upset stomach.

Areca palm

A pet-safe palm for indoors. Don't confuse it with the sago palm, which is very toxic.

Cactus

Not toxic, but the spines can injure the mouth, paws and eyes. Choose spineless types or keep them out of reach.

Catnip

Safe for cats and dogs, and most cats love it. Eating a lot can cause mild vomiting or diarrhea.

Orchid

Moth orchids (Phalaenopsis) are considered non-toxic. Nibbling can still cause a mild upset stomach; keep fertilizer and leaf-shine sprays away.

Pilea

Pilea peperomioides (Chinese money plant) is non-toxic to dogs and cats.

Rose

Roses are non-toxic, but the thorns can injure the mouth and paws. Garden roses may carry pesticides or fertilizer.

Wheatgrass

Cat grass (wheat, oat or barley grass) is safe and many cats like to chew it. Some cats vomit a little after eating grass, which is common.

This page is general information, not a substitute for a vet. Only a vet who knows your pet can judge the risk.
